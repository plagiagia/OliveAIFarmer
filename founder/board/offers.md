# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Two features in the stack solve real problems with money or deadlines attached. The δάκος alert protects the harvest itself. The ΟΣΔΕ/OPEKEPE field-logbook PDF meets a compulsory paperwork need. A grower can feel both of these, which is not true of "a digital record".
- The pricing fits how the buyer gets paid. A per-season price for a seasonal, lumpy income is the right instinct, and €49 a year is small next to the value of one saved harvest.
- The founder has already cut the tiers down once, so there is a habit of narrowing the offer.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A weak market for a solo seller.** The pain is real but comes once a season. A 200-tree family grove has little spare money. These buyers are older, offline and scattered, so they are hard to reach one at a time, and the market is not growing. This lens most often passes on market, and here market size, reachability and acquisition cost are all unknown.
2. **The value equation fails on likelihood and effort.** There is no proof the degree-day model catches fly outbreaks on a given grove, and no testimonials or season of results. A logbook asks the buyer to enter data all year before any payoff, which is the most effort a product can ask. The time to a result is a whole season.
3. **It sells features, not an outcome.** The paid tier is a list (alerts, AI, unlimited groves, cost-per-litre, PDF) with no promise, guarantee or name. Without a stated outcome the buyer compares it to free tools, an agronomist's phone call or the cooperative, and price becomes the only thing that sets it apart.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who has the bigger pain and more money: a 200-tree family, a 2,000-tree commercial grower, an accountant filing ΟΣΔΕ for 100 farmers, or a mill managing dozens of growers? (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors)
- What does the grower lose in euros from one missed δάκος spray window, and can you show it happened on real groves?
- How many minutes a week does the logbook ask of a grower, and can the accountant, the mill or a photo do that work instead?
- What would you guarantee? For example, "your ΟΣΔΕ logbook is accepted or your money back", or "a free season if we miss an outbreak".
- Could you charge €99 and still be the obvious choice for someone who would otherwise pay an agronomist? (/founder-cfo for cost to serve, /founder-consumer for willingness to pay)
- Which channel reaches 100 growers at once, such as cooperatives, mills, agronomists or accountants, and what does each grower cost to win? (/founder-competitors, /founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one buyer with real money and a short path to them, and test whether a B2B2C channel (mill, cooperative or accountant) beats selling direct.
2. Rebuild the paid tier around one named outcome, such as "never miss the δάκος window, ΟΣΔΕ paperwork done", with a guarantee the CFO says the business can afford.
3. Run one season of proof on 10 to 20 real groves (alerts sent compared with trap counts, PDFs accepted) before spending on acquisition.
4. Cut data entry at the start so the first value arrives in days, not at harvest.
